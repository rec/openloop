Too much history

For several years in the early 2000s, I organized a series of long-form electronic group improvisations called openLoop every Saturday afternoon in a small shop in the East Village called Chama that sold bee products and UFO books.

Decades before, the Village had been filled with such places, but this was one of the last, it was hopelessly uncommercial, it closed (we found out during the performance - here's what we were playing about half an hour after we found out it was over, I really cut loose) and I only did openLoop a few times in other places after that, as New York City continued its descent into capitalism and monetization.

I brought my electronic wind instrument, a digital board, a bunch of loopers and effects, and after a while, a CD recorder where I made live CDs of the mix: I have a couple of hundred hours of these recordings and they are quite a trip, like this.

A lot of musicians would show up and play, a half a dozen regulars. The music was good, audiences were small but warm.
Not many rules

Lots of suggestions!

You are not obliged to play or not to play
I mix or someone else does
Keep it going
Don't crowd
Think long-form
Remember the recording

The one rule was no acoustically loud instruments (what I think is called "silent stage" now) because it was a small room but, well, we never turned anyone away. (But we didn't get some guy with a huge guitar amp either.)
Backward into the future

America collapsed. We fled. Amsterdam was not a great place to meet musicians but Rouen is just crawling with musical talent.

Let's do it again but different and better.

I have an 18-channel 6-send digital mixer with 4 very good effects, unfortunately mixed by tablet, but it's light and very versatile.

And a few years ago, I wrote a program called recs that automatically records every channel of a digital board and throws away the silence. I've been using it for rehearsals very successfully for quite a while, and now I've been adding new features to it with, sigh, AI.

So I'm going to glue a little Raspberry Pi to the side of the mixer, plug in USB, and everything will be recorded, by which I mean not just the mix, but all the raw tracks individually, too, and then they'll automagically be uploaded to a server and be visible as the performance continues.
