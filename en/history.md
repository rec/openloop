# The history of openLoop

For several years starting in 2001, I, Tom Ritchford, organized a series of long-form
electronic group improvisations called openLoop every Saturday afternoon in a small shop
in the East Village called Chama that sold bee products and UFO books.

Decades before, the Village had been filled with such places, but this was one of the
last, it was hopelessly uncommercial, and it eventually closed.

We found out during a session that it was our last - here's what we were playing about
half an hour after we found out it was over.

We only did openLoop a few times in other places after that, as New York City continued
its descent into capitalism and monetization.

I would bring my trusty Yamaha WX7 electronic wind instrument, a digital board, a bunch
of synths, a tinny but expressive drum machines, lots of loopers and effects, and after
a while, a CD recorder where I made live CDs of the mix: I have a couple of hundred
hours of these recordings and they are quite a trip, like this.

A lot of musicians would show up and play, a half a dozen regulars. The music was good,
audiences were small but warm. No one became famous.
